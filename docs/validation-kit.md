# CareerLaunch Agent: Validation Kit

Owner: Member 1 (Jensen), though anyone on the team can run these. The goal is real evidence for the pitch, using no app code. Results go in `docs/business-case.md` under **Evidence to gather**.

## Ground rules

- Ask permission before taking notes, and before quoting anyone. Offer to keep them anonymous.
- **Never commit real names, résumés or contact details to this repo.** It is public. Record only anonymous summaries such as "junior, marketing."
- Don't promise anyone results, an interview or a job.
- Don't pitch during the interview. You're there to learn about their experience.

## Targets, set before we start

Setting these first stops us from reading whatever we want into the results.

| Test | Target | If we miss it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interviews | 5 of 10 students name a problem we solve as a top frustration | Rethink which problem we lead with |
| Trust | 5 of 10 say they've worried about overstating something or being caught out using AI | "Provably true" may not be the right lead message |
| Concierge test | 3 of 5 students say they'd use it again | Find out which part let them down |
| Career center | 1 staff member agrees to a follow-up or gives us a named problem | Rethink who pays |

## Test 1: Student interview (15 minutes)

Talk to students who have applied for internships recently, or are doing so now. Ask about what actually happened, not what they think they would do.

1. Tell me about the last internship you applied for. How did you find it?
2. Walk me through what you did from finding it to hitting submit.
3. What was the most frustrating part?
4. How many have you applied to, and how many were a waste of time? Why?
5. Have you ever missed a deadline or lost track of an application?
6. What tools do you use now? Have you used Simplify, Handshake or your career center?
7. Have you used AI on an application? How did it go?
8. Has AI ever made you sound better than you really are? Did that worry you?
9. If you could fix one thing about this process, what would it be?
10. Who else should I talk to about this?

**Notes template:** year and major, tools used, biggest frustration, a quote (with permission), and whether they'd try a test.

## Test 2: Concierge test (one student, about an hour)

Do the agent's job by hand before the app exists. This shows whether the service is valuable, and the results become real demo material.

1. Get the student's permission, and have them remove anything private from their résumé.
2. Using an AI assistant and real listings, find 5 internships and explain each match.
3. Check each résumé claim against what they've told you, and mark anything without proof.
4. Pick one real gap and suggest one small project that would close it.
5. Give them a one-page plan. They do all the applying themselves.
6. Ask: Did you learn anything new? What felt wrong? Would you use this again? What would you pay, if anything?

**Before you start:** if the AI tool stores what you paste in, remove names and contact details first.

## Test 3: Career-center conversation

**This is a draft. Nobody has sent it.** Fill in the blanks and send it from your own school email.

> Subject: Student project on internship applications: 15 minutes of your time?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm a student at [school] working on [class or competition], where our team is designing an AI assistant that helps students with internship applications while keeping them truthful.
>
> Could I ask you a few questions about how students use AI in their applications today? 15 minutes would be a huge help, and I'm happy to come to you.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

**Questions to ask**

1. Where do students struggle most, and where do you spend most of your time?
2. What tools does the career center provide now? What's working and what isn't?
3. How are students using AI in applications? What worries you about it?
4. Have employers said anything to you about AI-written applications?
5. If a tool showed proof behind each résumé claim, would that help you? Why or why not?
6. Who decides on tools like this, and what would they need to see?
7. What privacy rules would a student tool have to meet?

## Test 4: Try the competition (30 minutes)

Using a test profile, try Simplify's free version on a real listing, then check what Handshake offers at our school. Note what it does well and exactly where it stops. Add the results to `docs/competitor-research.md`.
