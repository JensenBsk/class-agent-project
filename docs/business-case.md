# CareerLaunch Agent: Business Case

Owner: Member 1 (Jensen).

**How to read this:** nothing here has been tested with customers yet. There are no market-size figures or prices because we have not sourced any, and any added later must link to a source.

## The problem

Students with real skills lose internship opportunities to the process itself. They don't know where to look, apply to poor matches, send generic résumés and miss deadlines.

Career centers can't close that gap on their own, because they don't have the staff to give every student individual support.

## The customer

Students use it, but they are unlikely to be the ones who pay.

| Group | Role | Why they would care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| College students | User | Less time spent, fewer missed deadlines, better applications |
| University career centers | Likely first buyer | Support more students without a matching increase in staff |
| Academic departments | Possible buyer | Better internship outcomes for their students |
| Workforce and first-generation student programs | Possible buyer | Structured support for a defined group |

## Why it is different

Job boards mostly list opportunities, and résumé tools mostly generate documents. CareerLaunch connects those steps in one workflow:

- Matching, documents, tracking and interview prep all use the same student profile and the same internship.
- Every recommendation explains its reasoning.
- The student approves every consequential step.
- It is built to keep applications truthful, and flags any claim it cannot find in the student's own material.

**Competitor check still to do:** this comparison comes from the brief and hasn't been checked. Before the pitch, compare at least three real products, including whatever career tools our own university already provides. That last one is the first question a career center will ask.

## Business model

**Recommended first model: annual university or career-center license**, because students are price-sensitive and institutions have a direct interest in career outcomes.

| Model | How it works | Why not first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freemium for students | Free matching and tracking, paid résumé and interview features | Students are price-sensitive |
| University license | Annual subscription, priced by enrollment | **Recommended first** |
| Career-center software | Advisor dashboards and progress summaries | Natural add-on to the license, but not in the prototype |
| Workforce program license | Access for a defined group of participants | Good later market |

**Pricing:** not set. We need at least one career-center conversation before putting a number in the pitch.

**Costs to work out:** AI usage per student, hosting, and privacy and security review. Universities are likely to ask how student data is protected before they buy.

## How we would measure success

The brief asks us to show how the prototype could measure results. The prototype doesn't have to collect all of these today.

| Stage | KPI | How it could be measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting started | Completed profiles | Profile saved with required fields |
| Engagement | Internships reviewed | Listing opened |
| Engagement | Interview prep sessions completed | Session finished |
| Progress | Applications prepared | Status reaches Ready for review |
| Progress | Applications submitted by users | Status reaches Applied |
| Progress | Deadline completion rate | Applied before deadline, out of those saved |
| Quality | Generated résumé content approved | Approved changes, out of those suggested |
| Value | Time saved and satisfaction | Short user survey |
| Outcome | Interview rate and offer rate | User-reported status changes over time |

**Rule for claims:** we can report what these numbers show, but we won't say CareerLaunch caused a student to get a job unless that was properly measured.

## Questions judges are likely to ask

| Question | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who pays? | Universities and career centers first, because they have budgets and care about outcomes |
| Why wouldn't a student just use ChatGPT? | A chatbot doesn't hold the whole process together: tracking, deadlines, consistent profile use, approvals and checks for invented claims |
| What stops it from lying on a résumé? | It flags claims it can't find in the student's material, and nothing is final until the student approves |
| Is the data real? | The prototype uses clearly labeled mock data, and the design allows real sources later |
| How do you know students want this? | **We need the evidence above to answer this honestly** |
